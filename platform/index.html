Housing

A home should be a foundation for independence, not a privilege protected by artificial scarcity. From Montiablo to Ventura, Convergence believes that everyone should have the opportunity to live affordably near their work, education, and community.

  • Build more, build freely.

    Liberalize zoning, permit higher-density and mixed-use developments near public transport, and streamline planning approvals.

  • Make land work.

    Keep land-value taxation to discourage speculation, and release suitable public and underused land for competitive development.

  • Plan better communities.

    Coordinate new housing with transport, schools, utilities, and public spaces, while protecting Caprica's natural environment and coastlines.

  • Expand choice and affordability.

    Encourage private investment, competition from across the Columbian Union, and modern construction methods, alongside public housing targets, housing assistance, and fair tenant protections.

Good ideas deserve an open door.

Back the people who take a chance, create something new, and move us all forward.

Business and enterprise

Caprica should reward initiative, not connections. Convergence believes in a competitive, predictable economy where businesses of every size can innovate, invest, and prosper.

  • Reward enterprise and investment.

    Establish a single 8% corporate tax, allow immediate deductions for productive capital investments, and provide tax relief for research and development.

  • Make starting up simple.

    Enable business registration within 24 hours, simplify tax filing, raise VAT exemptions for small enterprises, and allow startup losses to be carried forward.

  • Competition over privilege.

    Abolish politically negotiated tax exemptions, strengthen antitrust enforcement, remove unreasonable regional barriers, and open public procurement to fair competition.

  • Open Caprica to opportunity.

    Integrate the capital market across Columbia and remove barriers to enterprise across the Columbian Union.

Let opportunity travel.

Connect our communities, shorten the distance between us, and keep Caprica moving forward.

Infrastructure and transportation

Mobility is freedom. Convergence believes in a connected Caprica where reliable, affordable transport brings people closer to jobs, services, and opportunities, regardless of where they live.

  • One connected network.

    Break regional barriers. Introduce integrated ticketing across buses and ferries, coordinate timetables, and expand flexible transport services to underserved communities through targeted subsidies.

  • Build greener, more convenient.

    Expand safe cycling routes in the urban area and connect new housing developments to transport networks.

  • Open our gateways.

    Initiate major overhaul project on ports, airports, and freight infrastructure, promote competition among transport operators, this is to be done in coordination with the Columbian Union partners.

  • Fair and efficient investment.

    Prioritize projects through transparent cost-benefit assessments, competitive procurement, and long-term maintenance planning through proven public private collaboration models, reject local cronyism, while encouraging cleaner transport technologies through market liberal means.

Every mind, a world of possibility.

Give every generation the freedom to discover, the confidence to question, and the chance to go further.

Education and skills

Every Caprican deserves a fair start and the freedom to pursue their ambitions. Education funding should follow need, reward good teaching, and prepare students for a changing economy.

  • A fair start for every child.

    Fund universal preschool from age four, introduce needs-weighted school funding, and additional funding for disadvantaged pupils.

  • Trust teachers, raise standards.

    Fund 30 hours of paid annual training, mentor new teachers, and publish independently assessed school performance reports.

  • Value every pathway.

    Introduce employer-designed apprenticeships, subsidize vocational training placements, and establish transferable credits between technical colleges and universities.

  • Learning without borders.

    Protect free college tuition, expand universal recognition of online credentials, and secure mutual recognition of qualifications across the Columbian Union.

Care that sees the person.

A healthier Caprica begins with dignity, compassion, and the confidence that care will be there.

Healthcare

Healthcare should offer security without sacrificing choice. Convergence believes in universal coverage, competing providers, and a system that rewards better patient outcomes rather than more expensive treatments.

  • Care for everyone.

    Secure multipayer universal health insurance, subsidize premiums according to income, and cap annual out-of-pocket medical expenses.

  • Care when you need it.

    Guarantee primary-care appointments within seven days, expand pharmacists' prescribing powers, and fund mobile clinics in underserved communities.

  • Choice with accountability.

    Let accredited public and private providers deliver insured services at regulated rates, with published waiting times and risk-adjusted treatment outcomes.

  • Prevention and innovation.

    Cover preventive screenings and essential mental healthcare, introduce portable electronic health records, and require patient consent for nonclinical data sharing.

Your life, your voice, your freedom.

An open society makes room for different lives, different ideas, and equal dignity for all.

Civil liberties

Individual liberty requires enforceable rights and meaningful limits on state power. Convergence stands for a society where freedom is protected equally, including for those whose views we oppose.

  • Protect digital privacy.

    Mandate websites to reveal data use and access to private information. Require greater legal guardrail against accessing to internet communications.

  • Keep private lives private.

    Pass the privacy amendment in the constitution. Remove the loophole to mass surveillance and expansive internal securities.

  • Equal rights, equal dignity.

    Protect LGBT rights. Legislate on gender minorities' access to healthcare, public facilities, and employment protections.

  • Power under the law.

    Expand means-tested legal aid, establish independent police misconduct investigations, whistleblower protections, and require parliamentary renewal of emergency powers after 30 days.

An open Caprica in a connected world.

Build our strength, cultivate friendships, and advance Caprica's interests through patience, pragmatism, and peaceful engagement.

Foreign policy

Caprica's prosperity depends on an open and stable world. Convergence believes in principled but pragmatic diplomacy: avoiding unnecessary confrontation, building influence through economic success, and preserving our freedom to act independently.

  • Prosperity through openness.

    Deepen the Columbian Union's common market, negotiate reciprocal trade agreements, and diversify commercial partnerships to reduce strategic dependencies.

  • Friends without frontiers.

    Maintain diplomatic and economic relations across ideological divides, reject automatic alignment in external disputes or unnecessary saber rattling, and pursue cooperation wherever mutual interests exist.

  • Patience over confrontation.

    Establish maritime crisis hotlines, negotiate incident-prevention agreements in the Twin Strait, and prioritize discreet diplomacy and negotiated settlements over public ultimatums.

  • Strength without adventurism.

    Build long-term economic and diplomatic capabilities, avoid unnecessary foreign entanglements, and strengthen parliamentary approval for non-defensive military interventions.

Strong enough to keep the peace.

Protect the freedom we cherish and stand with the partners who share it.

Defense & Armed Forces

Convergence believes in a professional, technologically capable military designed to deter aggression, not invite confrontation. Defense spending must deliver measurable security rather than political prestige.

  • Protect our shores and skies.

    Prioritize maritime patrol aircraft, coastal surveillance, mine countermeasures, and integrated air-defense systems.

  • Readiness over appearances.

    Adopt five-year capability defense white paper, require competitive defense procurement, and publish independently audited expenditure and aggregate readiness reports.

  • Stronger together.

    Conduct joint exercises with Columbian Union partners, standardize military logistics and communications, and pursue shared procurement where cost-effective.

  • Force under civilian authority.

    Require parliamentary approval for overseas combat operations except immediate self-defense, prohibit domestic political surveillance by military intelligence, and conduct regular civilian oversight mechanisms.
